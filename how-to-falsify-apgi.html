How to Falsify APGI

Fifteen pre-registered predictions, each with a numeric threshold at which the corresponding component of the framework — or the framework as a whole — is disconfirmed. No hedging, no moving goalposts: this is the list of ways APGI is allowed to be wrong.

Why this page exists

A theory that cannot lose an argument isn't doing science. Every prediction below states what APGI says should happen, what the leading competing framework (GNWT, IIT, or predictive processing) says should happen instead, the experiment that tells them apart, what gets measured, and the pre-registered number at which APGI's claim is rejected.

Full statistical models and power analyses live in the APGI Protocols document and the OSF pre-registration linked above — this page is the index, not the primary source.

The 15 Predictions

Filter by epistemic tier. Tier 1 = thermodynamic (metabolic cost). Tier 2 = information-theoretic (precision, ignition probability). Tier 3 = computational (architecture, causal priority). Framework-level = all-or-nothing tests of APGI as a whole.

Bₜ

A note on how to read this: most rows carry a three-way outcome, not a binary one — a clear pass, a clear fail, and an indeterminate zone that triggers replication rather than a verdict. That's deliberate: it's what keeps this page honest rather than a highlight reel. Predictions 1–5 and 7 are testable now with existing TMS-EEG, fMRI, and pharmacological methods; Predictions 8–11 require intracranial access; Predictions 6, 14, and 15 (Tier 1, metabolic) are the framework's acknowledged weakest evidential tier, unmeasured by direct calorimetry as of this writing.
