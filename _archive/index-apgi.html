The Framework

The APGI Framework models consciousness as a threshold-crossing event in a predictive processing system. Three dimensions interact: threshold sensitivity (how easily your system activates), interoceptive precision (accuracy of internal state estimation), and prediction error magnitude (how much reality violates expectation). When precision-weighted errors exceed your dynamic gate, consciousness broadcasts.

Parameter 1
Bₜ
Threshold Sensitivity
The thalamic gating threshold. How much signal must accumulate before consciousness activates. Lower threshold = hair-trigger awareness. Higher threshold = requires major disruptions.
Measured in precision-weighted units
Parameter 2
Π
Interoceptive Precision
Confidence in your internal state estimates. Mediated by anterior insula & ACC. High precision = accurate body reading. Low precision = noisy internal signals.
Interoceptive accuracy
Parameter 3
|ε|
Prediction Error
Mismatch between expectation and reality. Detected by anterior insula & superior temporal sulcus. Larger errors drive consciousness. The surprise signal.
Magnitude of surprise

How It Works

The APGI system operates in real time. Your brain continuously estimates its own state, compares predictions to incoming signals, and gates information to consciousness when surprises exceed your threshold. This isn't sequential—it's recursive, with feedback loops enabling recalibration.

Step 1 — Prediction
Your brain predicts internal state. Based on prior experience and current context, the cortex generates a predictive model of what sensations and states should occur next.
Predictive model visualization
Step 2 — Error Detection
Incoming signals reveal error. Actual interoceptive signals (heart rate, temperature, tension) arrive. Precision-weighted prediction errors are computed in insular and anterior cingulate regions.
Error signal cascade
Step 3 — Threshold Crossing
Global workspace ignites. When errors exceed the dynamic gate, the frontoparietal global workspace broadcasts. Consciousness emerges. Your awareness is now engaged with the prediction violation.
Broadcast activation

Why This Matters

Application
Anxiety
Anxiety is a miscalibrated threshold. The gate is set too low. Minor prediction errors—a text message delay, a slightly elevated heart rate—trigger consciousness and broadcast as threat. The mechanism: sustained norepinephrine reduces the thalamic gate threshold (Aston-Jones & Cohen, 2005).
Intervention: Raise the threshold. Meditation reduces norepinephrine. Beta-blockers lower arousal baseline. Threshold lowering sequences increase error tolerance.
Application
Flow States
Flow is precision optimization + threshold matching. When challenge matches skill (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990), errors are predictable and well-modeled. Precision is maximized. Consciousness is fully absorbed—not distracted by unexpected errors.
Intervention: Optimize precision. Reduce distractions. Increase feedback frequency. Match task difficulty to skill level.

Model Integrity & Scientific Limitations

All APGI software outputs are model-based estimates derived from behavioral proxy signals. They are not direct neural recordings and do not constitute clinical measurements. The framework is a theoretical model inspired by predictive processing literature; it has not been independently validated against ground-truth neural data at publication. All quantitative outputs should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating, not diagnostic.
