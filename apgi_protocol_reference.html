EP-0
HEP Proxy Validation
Does the heartbeat-evoked potential measure interoceptive precision?
🫀
Plain English

Every time your heart beats, your brain produces a tiny electrical response called the heartbeat-evoked potential (HEP). APGI proposes that the size of this response is a proxy for how much trust your brain is placing in body signals — its "interoceptive precision" (Πⁱ). This protocol checks whether that idea holds up. Experiment P0b is particularly elegant: give participants the drug physostigmine (which boosts acetylcholine, the precision neuromodulator) and measure whether HEP amplitude rises by at least 15%. If it does, you've demonstrated a direct pharmacological handle on interoceptive precision. P0c adds an fMRI link: does the anterior insula — the region encoding Πⁱ — light up in proportion to the HEP on every single trial?

P0a HEP amplitude correlates with an independent interoceptive precision index r(HEP, interoceptive d′) > 0.35, p < 0.01 r < 0.20 across two independent samples
P0b Physostigmine (ACh↑) raises HEP amplitude ≥15% vs. placebo (d ≥ 0.50, BF₁₀ ≥ 10) HEP ↑ ≥15% vs. placebo (d ≥ 0.50, BF₁₀ ≥ 10) No HEP change despite verified ACh elevation (confirmed by pupil constriction)
P0c Anterior insula BOLD tracks HEP trial-by-trial r(HEP amplitude, aINS BOLD) > 0.30 within participants aINS BOLD and HEP statistically independent after arousal covariate

EP-1
EEG Gating
Does interoceptive focus amplify the P3b ignition signal?
🧠
Plain English

The P3b is a brainwave spike (~300–500 ms after a stimulus) that marks conscious access — the moment a signal "ignites" into awareness. APGI says interoceptive precision (Πⁱ) gates this event: when you're tuned into your body, faint stimuli are more likely to reach awareness. EP-1 tests this by comparing P3b amplitude across three attention conditions — focusing on body sensations, on external stimuli, or splitting attention. APGI predicts the body-focus condition produces the biggest P3b for near-threshold stimuli. P1b adds a cardiac-phase test: stimuli presented during diastole (the quiet phase between heartbeats, when interoceptive prediction errors peak) should be detected more often. P1c checks whether people who are naturally good at sensing their own heartbeat (high interoceptive accuracy, IA) show stronger versions of all these effects.

P1a-trait High interoceptive accuracy (IA) individuals show greater perceptual sensitivity in interoceptive condition High-IA d-prime > Low-IA (d ≥ 0.5), specific to interoceptive condition High/Low-IA do not differ on interoceptive d-prime (d < 0.3)
P1b Stimuli presented during cardiac diastole are detected more often than during systole Diastole vs. systole hit-rate advantage ≥ 5% Uniform detection rates across cardiac phases (interaction p > 0.15)

EP-2
Causal TMS
Can we break interoceptive gating by disrupting the insula?
⚡
Plain English

Correlations show that brain regions are associated with a process. TMS (transcranial magnetic stimulation) proves causation by temporarily disrupting a region and measuring what breaks. EP-2 uses TMS on two targets: the anterior insula (encodes Πⁱ) and the dlPFC/posterior parietal cortex (the frontoparietal ignition network). APGI predicts these are dissociable — insula TMS should specifically knock out the HEP–P3b coupling (the interoceptive gating mechanism) while leaving externally-driven ignition intact. dlPFC TMS should reduce overall ignition capacity (measured by PCI — a TMS-based complexity index) without touching the HEP. If both sites produce identical disruption patterns, the dissociation claim fails.

CodeClaimConfirmsFalsifies
P2a Insula TMS reduces PCI ~20%; dlPFC/PPC TMS reduces PCI 15–25%; HEP–PCI correlation abolished by insula TMS only Dissociable PCI reductions; HEP–PCI coupling disappears under insula TMS, survives dlPFC TMS No PCI change vs. vertex; or equivalent PCI reduction at vertex control site
P2b Insula TMS selectively disrupts interoceptive gating without suppressing exteroceptive P3b Insula TMS: HEP–P3b coupling < 0.15; exteroceptive P3b spared. dlPFC TMS: HEP unaffected Uniform suppression of both P3b streams; or HEP equally reduced by dlPFC TMS
P2c High-IA individuals show strongest insula TMS disruption Accuracy × TMS site interaction on PCI (p < 0.05, η² > 0.10) No interaction between baseline IA accuracy and TMS site

EP-3
Agent Simulation
Does an APGI computational agent outperform simpler models on the Iowa Gambling Task?
🤖
Plain English

Before running expensive human experiments, build a computer agent that uses the APGI equations and test it on the Iowa Gambling Task (IGT) — a classic decision-making paradigm where optimal play requires learning from gut-level signals about risky decks. The APGI agent has a somatic marker module (β_SM) that learns to weight interoceptive signals. EP-3 asks: does it converge to good performance within the same ~50–80 trials humans need? And do its internal dynamics match APGI's theory — does the somatic marker readout (M̂) precede threshold crossing (θₜ), as the causal model predicts? P3e delivers the toughest test: does the full APGI model fit held-out human IGT data better than simpler predictive processing or Global Workspace models (by Bayesian BIC ≥ 10)?

P3b Post-ignition action selection entropy increases; ignitions dominated by interoceptive component Post-ignition entropy > pre-ignition (p < 0.05); ≥70% of ignitions have high interoceptive component Ignition uncorrelated with adaptation
P3c Somatic marker (M̂) temporally precedes threshold crossing (θₜ) Cross-correlation peak at negative lag; M̂ leads by ≥ 1 trial in ≥ 75% of ignition events M̂ simultaneous with or lagging θₜ crossing

EP-4
DoC Biomarker
Can HEP + PCI predict consciousness recovery in disorders of consciousness?
🏥
Plain English

This is APGI's most clinically urgent protocol. Patients in vegetative state (VS), minimally conscious state (MCS), or emerging from disorders of consciousness are often misclassified — VS patients may be covertly aware. EP-4 tests whether two APGI parameters — HEP amplitude (interoceptive precision Πⁱ) and PCI (perturbational complexity index, a TMS-EEG measure of ignition capacity) — can predict whether a patient will recover consciousness at 3- and 6-month follow-up, and do so better together than either alone. P4c uses a cold-pressor test (CPT) as an interoceptive perturbation: MCS patients should show a PCI increase in response to the somatic stimulus; VS/UWS patients should not, providing a bedside dissociation test.

P4b HEP amplitude distinguishes MCS from VS/UWS at group level; four-group gradient confirmed HEP MCS > VS/UWS (p < 0.05, d > 0.5) No significant HEP difference between MCS and VS/UWS
P4c Interoceptive perturbation (CPT) increases PCI in MCS but not VS/UWS; PCI change correlates with HEP change PCI ↑ ≥ 10% in MCS post-CPT; VS/UWS no change; r(ΔPCI, ΔHEP) > 0.4 No PCI change in MCS post-CPT; or equivalent change in VS/UWS

EP-5
fMRI Anticipation
Does vmPFC encode anticipated somatic value — not sensory outcome?
🔮
Plain English

APGI predicts that the ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) acts as a somatic marker predictor — it activates during the anticipation of a body-relevant outcome based on expected value (EV), not in response to the physical sensation itself. This is a sharp mechanistic claim: vmPFC is computing Πⁱ × EV before the event, not reacting to a skin conductance response (SCR) after it. EP-5 uses an fMRI gambling task with variable foreperiods (anticipation windows) to test this. The critical double-dissociation: vmPFC BOLD should correlate with EV during anticipation but not with SCR at outcome; vmPFC–posterior insula connectivity should peak during anticipation and drop at outcome. P5d is the cleanest control: remove the anticipation window entirely (0 ms foreperiod) and vmPFC–insula coupling should collapse.

P5a vmPFC BOLD modulated by expected value during anticipation; does NOT correlate with outcome SCR vmPFC anticipation BOLD modulated by EV (p < 0.05 FWE); vmPFC–SCR outcome correlation r < 0.2 vmPFC BOLD correlates significantly with outcome-locked SCR (r > 0.35)
P5b vmPFC–posterior insula functional connectivity peaks during anticipation, not outcome PPI coefficient vmPFC–pIC: anticipation > outcome (p < 0.05 FWE) No anticipatory vmPFC–pIC coupling; connectivity limited to outcome period
P5c vmPFC anticipation effect driven by valence (EV), not sensory contrast vmPFC activation modulated by EV (p < 0.05 FWE); no sensory contrast effect vmPFC activation correlates with sensory contrast regardless of valence
P5d Eliminating anticipation window (0 ms foreperiod) collapses vmPFC–posterior insula coupling vmPFC–pIC PPI: long foreperiod > no foreperiod (p < 0.05) vmPFC–insula coupling persists at equivalent magnitude in no-anticipation condition

EP-6
iEEG Dynamics
Does frontoparietal cortex show bistable, phase-transition ignition dynamics?
📡
Plain English

Intracranial EEG (iEEG) — recorded from electrodes placed directly on the cortex during neurosurgical procedures — gives access to high-gamma power (70–150 Hz), the best human proxy for local neural firing. APGI's bifurcation theory makes a specific prediction: near-threshold stimuli should push frontoparietal high-gamma into a bimodal distribution — clustering into either "high" or "low" states, not a smooth continuum. This is the empirical signature of a phase transition (bistability). P6c tests a subtler prediction: in the 500 ms before a detected stimulus, autocorrelation (AC1) of high-gamma should increase monotonically — a phenomenon called "critical slowing" that occurs when a dynamical system approaches a tipping point. P6d checks the transit time: near-threshold trials should linger in intermediate states for >100 ms before committing, rather than snapping immediately into high or low.

Predictions & Evidence Criteria
CodeClaimConfirmsFalsifies
P6a Frontoparietal high-gamma power shows bimodal (not graded) distribution at near-threshold Hartigan dip test p < 0.05; 2-component Gaussian ΔBIC > 10 vs. 1-component Unimodal continuous distribution; dip test p > 0.20
P6b Bimodality is specific to frontoparietal ignition network, not occipital regions Region × bimodality-index interaction significant (p < 0.05); frontoparietal dip statistic > occipital Uniform bimodality across all regions; or uniform graded responses
P6c AC1 of pre-ignition high-gamma increases monotonically in 500 ms before detected stimuli AC1 Kendall τ > 0.3 in detected trials; absent or reversed in non-detected trials AC1 flat or decreasing before detected stimuli
P6d Near-threshold stimuli produce intermediate high-gamma states lasting >100 ms before committing Proportion of near-threshold trials with intermediate state >100 ms duration > 15% Rapid commitment to high/low state within 50 ms in ≥ 85% of near-threshold trials
